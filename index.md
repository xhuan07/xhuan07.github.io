## About Me

I am a Beckman Institute Postdoctoral Fellow at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, where I work with Prof. Ying Diao. I develop microscale 3D printing methods for functional polymers. My research asks how flow, evaporation, and adhesion during printing determine the color, motion, and electrical response of printed structures.

I received my Ph.D. in Mechanical Engineering from the University of Hong Kong in 2023 under the supervision of Prof. Ji Tae Kim. I earned my B.Eng. in Mechatronic Engineering from Zhejiang University in 2019.

## Research

### Automated measurements and closed-loop printing

How do cracks in a printed polymer film affect its ability to carry current as it stretches? I built an automated platform that synchronizes a motorized stretching stage, a camera, and a digital multimeter. I can track where cracks form and how the film's electrical resistance changes during the same experiment. I am also developing a direct ink writing system that will use in situ measurements to adjust printing conditions and control structural color.

### Printing structural color

I developed a direct ink writing method that changes the color of a printed bottlebrush block copolymer pillar by varying the printing speed. Faster printing reduces the spacing between polymer domains and shifts the reflected color toward blue. To study how these domains form and orient within a pillar, I served as lead researcher for X-ray scattering experiments at beamline 12-ID-B of the Advanced Photon Source at Argonne National Laboratory.

### Printing soft microactuators

During my Ph.D., I developed a method to print two materials side by side through a double-barreled theta pipette, forming a bilayer microactuator in a single step. By adjusting the printing conditions, I could tune the adhesion between the two layers and control how the printed structure moved in response to a stimulus.
